[STEP 3: CAPTURING THE HANDSHAKE - THE CRITICAL MOMENT]

This is the part most people get wrong. Wi-Fi security works by a 'handshake'. When you connect your phone or laptop to a Wi-Fi network, your device and the router exchange a small piece of data to prove they both know the password. This is the handshake. We cannot crack the password without capturing this specific moment.

Here is how we trigger the handshake. We will use a tool called 'airodump-ng' with a filter. Type 'airodump-ng -c <channel number> --bssid <BSSID> wlan0mon'. Replace the placeholders with your actual numbers. This tells the software to focus only on your target router.

Now, how do we get the handshake? We will use 'aireplay-ng'. This tool can deauthenticate a connected device. Type 'aireplay-ng -0 10 -a <BSSID> wlan0mon'. The '-0' means deauthentication, the '10' means do it 10 times, and '-a' is the access point. **Note: The '-c client MAC' flag is optional and used to target a specific connected device. If you omit this, the command deauths all connected clients.** What this does is kick a specific connected device off the network. When that device tries to reconnect, the handshake happens instantly. We capture it.

Here is the everyday analogy: Imagine a bouncer at a club. The handshake is the ID check. We want to catch the moment the new person shows their ID. By deauthing the client, we force them to re-enter, and we catch that ID check. It is quick, it is ethical because we are testing our own network, and it works to force a reconnection if a client is present and within range.
